# Pixel 9a - after tuning (BBR)

> Carrier backfilled on 2026-09-05 from the
> [Google Fi record](2026-09-05-pixel-9a-google-fi.md), which confirmed the same
> phone and session. It was not recorded at test time; `carrier.network` is
> still unknown. No measurement was changed.

Same phone, same cable, same session as
[2026-09-05-pixel-9a-cubic](2026-09-05-pixel-9a-cubic.md). Only the host TCP
settings changed.

## Setup

Applied on the host, persisted in `/etc/sysctl.d/99-tether.conf` with
`/etc/modules-load.d/bbr.conf` loading `tcp_bbr` at boot:

    net.ipv4.tcp_congestion_control = bbr
    net.ipv4.tcp_slow_start_after_idle = 0
    net.ipv4.tcp_mtu_probing = 1

These are system-wide, not tether-scoped. `fq_codel` left alone - BBR has
carried its own pacing since 4.13.

## Observations

Single-stream went 15/44/116 to 125/153/106 Mbps. The spread collapsed from 7.8x
to 1.4x. The floor moved most: worst case 15 to 106 Mbps, roughly 7x. Peak 153
Mbps now exceeds the 133 Mbps best seen under CUBIC and approaches the 211 Mbps
four-stream aggregate, which is the expected shape if the diagnosis was right -
one flow no longer needs three companions to fill the link.

RTT was checked specifically to confirm BBR was not buying throughput with queue
depth: 29.6 to 29.7 ms avg, mdev 4.1 to 5.8. No bufferbloat.

## Issues

None.

## Follow-ups

The 4-stream parallel test was **not** re-run after the change, so
`parallel_4_aggregate_mbps` is deliberately blank rather than carried over from
the CUBIC record. Worth running to see whether aggregate also rose or whether
single-stream simply caught up to an unchanged ceiling.

USB 2.0 cable remains the outstanding ceiling, and it matters more now: 153 Mbps
single against a realistic USB 2.0 bulk limit near 300 Mbps.
